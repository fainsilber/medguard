MedGuard Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 September 2026

The short version

  • MedGuard keeps a household’s medication records: what was given, when, and by whom. That is sensitive health information, and this policy is written with that in mind.
  • Records live on your own device first. They only leave it if you set up or join a household, so the caregivers in it can share them.
  • No ads. No analytics or tracking. No accounts with an email address or password. We never sell or rent your information.
  • You can delete a household, and everything on our servers with it, from inside the app.

1. Who this policy covers

MedGuard is a medication-tracking app for households caring for someone on a complex medication schedule. It is available as a web app (installable to your home screen) at medguard-web.fainsilber.workers.dev and as an Android app (il.co.fainsilber.med). This policy covers both apps and the MedGuard server they connect to.

MedGuard is developed and operated by Johnny Fainsilber (“we”, “us”). Contact: medguard@fainsilber.co.il.

2. How MedGuard works

On its own, MedGuard stores everything on the device you are holding and works fully offline. In this mode your records are not sent to us. The one thing the app may still do is ask the MedGuard server for the current time, to check that your device’s clock is right before it approves a dose; that request carries no records.

In a household, you start a household, or join one with a six-digit code from a caregiver who already belongs. From then on the records are also stored on the MedGuard server, so every caregiver’s device stays in step, receives live updates, and can be sent reminders.

4. What we do not collect

  • No email addresses, phone numbers, passwords, payment details, dates of birth or government ID numbers.
  • No access to your device’s location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • No advertising identifiers, and no analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting software in either app.

5. How we use information

  • To show your household’s records and keep every caregiver’s device in step.
  • To run dosing safety checks. For example, the server re-checks a dose against its cooldown and daily limit so that two caregivers cannot both log the same dose.
  • To send dose reminders, to escalate to the whole household when a dose has not been confirmed, and to send low-stock and Shabbat alerts.
  • To keep the service secure, for example by limiting repeated attempts to guess a join code.
  • To diagnose and fix faults.

We do not use information for advertising, marketing or profiling, and we do not sell it.

6. Notifications

Dose alerts name the person, the medicine and the amount, for example “Sam · Methotrexate: dose due”, because a caregiver woken at 3 a.m. needs to know who it is for and what to give. That means a person’s name and a medicine name can appear on a locked screen and stay in your phone’s notification history until they are cleared. Anyone who can see your phone can see it. You can turn notifications off at any time in your device or browser settings.

  • Android: dose alarms are scheduled on the phone itself. As a backstop, the server can also send a message through Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM). To do that, the server stores your device’s FCM registration token, Google’s messaging software creates an identifier for your installation of the app, and the text of each message, including the person’s name, the medicine name and the dose, passes through Google’s servers.
  • Web: the server stores your browser’s push subscription (an address at your browser maker’s push service, plus public encryption keys) and sends alerts through that service. The message content is encrypted so that the push service cannot read it.

7. Android permissions

The Android app asks for the following. None of them gives us access to your personal information; they exist so a dose alarm reliably rings.

  • Notifications: to show dose alerts.
  • Exact alarms: to ring at the minute a dose is due.
  • Run at startup: to set the alarms again after the phone restarts.
  • Foreground service: to play the alert sound for its full length, and to record a tap on “Taken” or “Snooze” from a locked screen.
  • Full-screen alerts and Do Not Disturb access: so an unconfirmed dose can get through when the phone is locked or silenced. Android asks you to grant Do Not Disturb access yourself.
  • Battery optimisation exemption: so Android does not stop the alarms to save power.

9. How we protect information

  • All traffic between the apps and the server uses HTTPS.
  • Every request is tied to the household its device token belongs to, and the server only ever returns that household’s data, never another’s. Device tokens and join codes are stored only as one-way hashes; a join code expires after 15 minutes and works once.
  • On Android, app data is excluded from cloud backups, so the dosing history is not copied to your Google account, and the access token is kept in Android’s Keystore-backed secure storage.

Limits, stated plainly. MedGuard does not add its own encryption to stored records, on your device or on our server. It relies on your device’s encryption (use a screen lock) and on the encryption Cloudflare provides for its storage. A phone that is unlocked and shared with others shows whatever MedGuard shows. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee that this one is.

10. Keeping and deleting information

  • On your device, records stay until you remove them: use “Clear all data on this device” on the Log screen, use “Leave this household” on the Household screen (which clears this device’s copy), clear the app’s storage, or uninstall.
  • On our server, a household’s information is kept for as long as the household exists. To delete it, open the Household screen and choose “Delete this household”. That permanently erases the household, its caregivers and devices, every medicine, schedule, dose log, stock record and setting, and the reminder-scheduling data derived from them, for every caregiver. It cannot be undone. Copies already saved on caregivers’ own devices stay there until they are cleared on each device.
  • Removing one device: “Revoke” on the Household screen deletes that device’s record, including its notification address.
  • Individual dose entries cannot be edited or deleted. A dosing history that could be quietly rewritten would not be a trustworthy medical record, so a correction is recorded as a new entry that supersedes the old one. Deleting the household is the way to remove the history from our server.
  • If you cannot use the app to delete, for example because no device is still signed in, email us at medguard@fainsilber.co.il. Because deletion is permanent and affects every caregiver, we may ask you to show that you belong to the household first.
  • IP addresses: when someone tries to join a household, the address is kept for about ten minutes to limit repeated guessing, then discarded. Cloudflare may keep its own operational logs of connections under its own policy.

11. Your choices

  • Use MedGuard on a single device without ever creating or joining a household.
  • Export your records at any time from the Log screen: a CSV, a full JSON backup, and on the web a printable summary.
  • Correct a record by adding a corrected entry, or by editing a medicine or schedule.
  • Turn notifications off in your device or browser settings.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have further rights over your personal information, such as access or deletion. Email us and we will help.

12. Children

MedGuard is for adult caregivers. The records in it often concern a child in a caregiver’s care, and that information is entered by the caregiver; the app does not ask for a child’s date of birth or any ID number, only a display name you choose. MedGuard is not directed at children, and a child should not create a household. If you believe one has, email us and we will delete it.

13. Where information is processed

Cloudflare runs a global network, so information held on our server may be processed and stored in countries other than your own.

14. Changes to this policy

If we change how MedGuard handles information, we will update this page and the date at the top. Please check back if you rely on it.
